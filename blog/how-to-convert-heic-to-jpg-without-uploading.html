How to Convert HEIC to JPG Without Uploading Your Photos

2026-09-29 · 6 min read · PixMint Blog

If you own an iPhone, your photos are probably HEIC. If you've ever tried to open them on a Windows PC, attach them to a form, or send them to a print shop, you already know the problem: half the world still can't read HEIC. So you search "heic to jpg" — along with roughly 246,000 other people every month in the US alone — and land on a converter site.

Here's the part most of those sites won't tell you: the moment you drop a photo on them, it gets uploaded to their server. Your kid's face, your ID scan, the GPS coordinates in the EXIF — all of it travels to a machine you don't control, under a privacy policy you didn't read.

This guide covers three ways to convert HEIC to JPG, what each one actually does with your photos, and how to do the whole thing without any upload at all.

Option 1: Online converter sites (the privacy trap)

The top results for "heic to jpg" are almost all server-side converters. The flow is always the same: upload → wait in a queue → download. It works, but consider what you're agreeing to:

  • Your photos are stored — at least temporarily — on third-party infrastructure, often in another country
  • Free tiers usually cap you at a handful of files per day, or watermark the output
  • Batch conversions of a full camera roll are slow, because every megabyte has to make a round trip over the network

For a meme, fine. For a photo of your passport? Think twice.

Option 2: OS-level tools (safe, but clunky)

On Windows

Microsoft's Photos app can open HEIC if you install the "HEIF Image Extensions" codec from the Microsoft Store, then export as JPG. It works offline and your photos stay put — but the codec sometimes costs a dollar, installs system-wide, and batch conversion is manual, file by file.

On a Mac

Preview opens HEIC natively: File → Export → Format: JPEG. Private and simple for one or two photos, but again, no real batch workflow.

Option 3: Convert in your browser, no upload (recommended)

Modern browsers are powerful enough to decode HEIC locally using WebAssembly. That's what PixMint's HEIC to JPG converter does: it ships a WASM build of libheif (the same decoder operating systems use) to your browser tab, and the conversion happens entirely on your device.

You can verify the "no upload" claim yourself: load the page, turn off your Wi-Fi, and convert. It still works — because nothing was ever sent anywhere.

One honest caveat: the file-size tax

HEIC is roughly half the size of an equivalent JPG — that's exactly why Apple chose it. Independent benchmarks put a quality-matched JPG at about 2x the HEIC file size, and a lossless PNG at nearly 5x. So convert for compatibility, not by default: keep your archive in HEIC, and convert the copies you actually need to send, print, or upload.

The bottom line

HEIC to JPG is a solved problem — the real question is who gets to see your photos along the way. Server-side converters: everyone. OS tools: nobody, but tedious. A browser-based, on-device converter gives you the convenience of the former with the privacy of the latter.
